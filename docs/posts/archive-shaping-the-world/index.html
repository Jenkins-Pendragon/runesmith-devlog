Shaping the world

Terrain, ruins, water and a visual palette: a look back at the landscape that came before the crafting loop.

From the development archive. This entry describes the game at the time of the work.

Image note: Cover captured October 1, 2026. The cover is a later-build illustration, not a screenshot from this development period. Current prefab comparisons below are dated individually.

I started with the ground beneath the game. Before a resource could be mined or a bench could be built, I needed a landscape that could hold those actions and a visual direction I could keep working with.

A landscape and a palette

The first terrain work moved through sculpting, tiled terrain experiments, grass rendering and revised rock textures. Lighting and post-processing changed alongside the materials. Later terrain stamps and a broader base world gave me more ways to shape places rather than repeat the same patch of ground.

I also prepared an environment library: meadows, mountains, glaciers, volcanic pieces, foliage, water, temples and ruins. Some of that work was content preparation rather than a finished playable location. Ruin pieces and god statues began to give the scenes recognizable landmarks.

The Artemis landmark overlooking the landscape. Later-build illustration, not a screenshot from the development period. Captured October 1, 2026.

Atmosphere around the work

Waterfall shaders, custom toon materials and ambient occlusion helped establish the look. Wind, leaves, dust, fog and firefly assets expanded the atmosphere toolkit; importing those assets was groundwork, not a claim that every effect was already active everywhere.

The world-scan effect was integrated as the environment developed. Lava materials and scene settings received later adjustments, while shop pieces and statues connected the visual palette to places a player could recognize.

The hideout and its surrounding architecture. Later-build illustration, not a screenshot from the development period. Captured October 1, 2026.

Looking back, this phase was about finding a world worth returning to. The later gathering and workshop systems were built on top of these terrain and art decisions.
