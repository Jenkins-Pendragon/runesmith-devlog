Tools, a character and a place to work

The early tools, character art and everyday shortcuts that gave gathering and crafting a human scale.

From the development archive. This entry describes the game at the time of the work.

Image note: Cover captured October 2, 2026. The cover is a later-build illustration, not a screenshot from this development period. Current prefab comparisons below are dated individually.

A crafting game needs more than item definitions. I needed tools that looked usable, a character who could perform the work and interfaces that stayed readable around both.

Giving the work a shape

Hand-tool models, human walking and crafting animations, and a character material pass formed the early foundation. Brewing workbench art, a statue and small supporting props broadened the library around the crafting spaces. Interface masking and blur assets were prepared alongside them.

Brewing workbench variants. Staged current-prefab comparison, captured October 2, 2026. Left to right: tier 1, tier 2, tier 3.

The brewing comparison shows how much of a station's identity sits in its silhouette: bottles, shelving and the growing frame tell a different story at each tier. These are current models shown together to illustrate the art, rather than a reconstruction of the first build.

Character choice and quick access

Later work added male and female textures and model selection, with character setup following the chosen model. Tool icons, hand tools and statue materials received another pass as the rune systems grew.

A radial shortcut gave the inventory and rune page a quick route from the world. Cursor management followed the active interface, and font atlas work supported the text those screens needed to show.

Tailoring workbench variants. Staged current-prefab comparison, captured October 2, 2026. Left to right: tier 1, tier 2, tier 3.

This was the supporting layer around the larger systems: recognizable objects, a coherent character and less friction when moving from the world into a menu.
